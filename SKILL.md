# Interweave Photo Abstraction

> **License notice:** This skill is licensed for personal, non-commercial use only. Whenever the skill, a modified version, or work materially created with it is published or shared, credit **LeoLittleLeo** and link to the [original GitHub repository](https://github.com/LeoLittleLeo/interweave-photo-abstraction). Commercial use is prohibited. See [LICENSE](LICENSE) for the complete terms.

Create an authored poster whose central aesthetic is **ordered interweaving of reality and abstraction**. Contrast comes from photographic and non-photographic regions entering, stopping, and reappearing across the scene. Order comes from a limited palette, disciplined boundaries, restrained media, and object-level consistency: every living subject is wholly photographic or wholly abstract, while major nonliving architecture and natural scenery deliberately contain both photographic evidence and abstract reconstruction.

## Workflow

1. Inspect the source and metadata. Diagnose the scene before choosing a style: scene type, primary subject, identity sensitivity, face clarity, tall anchors, dominant regions, palette, negative space, and reliable capture date.
2. Read [references/visual-direction.md](references/visual-direction.md). When the scene contains a living cluster, an architecture-led framed view, disposable interior furniture, or a dominant botanical group, also read [references/exemplars.md](references/exemplars.md) and inspect only the routed exemplar image(s). Use exemplars as structural and quality references, never as fixed palettes or composition templates. Then state one source-grounded artistic proposition: a relationship, tension, transition, or emotional fact that the poster will clarify. Do not invent a story unsupported by the image.
3. Write a compact strategy record using the schema below. Make preservation, omission, reconstruction, echo color, negative space, and copy all serve the proposition and remain traceable to visible evidence or an explicit user request.
4. Choose `light`, `medium`, or `strong` abstraction. Respect a user-requested intensity; otherwise use `medium` with a 45–60% reconstructed-area target. Lower only for identity-sensitive documentary portraits; raise for robust silhouettes, scenery, distant figures, or explicit permission.
5. Assign each visible living subject one coherent treatment. Use `face-locked, body-flexible, no-source-composite` for the primary person by default and `fully abstract` only for distant/nonessential faceless people or explicit identity reinterpretation. Keep facial identity and semantic pose coherent while allowing the body, clothing, peripheral hair, and edges to be regenerated or integrated.
6. Assign every major nonliving built or landscape region a controlled real/abstract division. Preserve a calm photographic recognition core and reconstruct a distinct contiguous portion; avoid tiny alternating patches. Plan 3–6 large semantic masses, a limited shared palette, one concentrated echo color at most, and only justified optional modules.
7. Generate or edit the complete artwork as one integrated composition. Preserve the primary person's facial identity at approximately 95% perceptual fidelity: facial geometry, expression, gaze, eyewear, hairline, jaw, and distinctive features must remain immediately recognizable. This target applies primarily to the face, not to whole-person pixels or silhouette. Exact source pixels are not required and must never be restored by source-person compositing.
8. Let final artistic coherence take priority outside core facial identity. Permit restrained changes to garment color, garment edges, lighting, texture, peripheral hair, body contour, and local silhouette transitions when they improve palette integration, material continuity, or the real/abstract thesis. Preserve pose, anatomy, gesture, and meaningful contact relationships.
9. Compare the candidate final side by side with the source at matched face scale and against the coded quality gate. Judge facial fidelity perceptually using the identity rubric; judge the complete image by composition, abstraction, boundary design, palette, finish, and absence of accidental compositing seams. If the person region fails, regenerate or locally re-edit it; never repair it by pasting source pixels back.
10. Use `scripts/inspect_photo.py` for metadata when useful. Use `scripts/compose_poster.py` in default `processed` mode for the orientation-adaptive editorial collage, explicit `fullbleed` mode for an edge-to-edge rectangle, or `postcard` mode for an original/generated comparison.

## Rule Priority

Typography is subordinate to the primary subject, real/abstract composition, major color masses, and negative-space structure. It completes the layout and guides the eye toward its exit point; it must never become the primary visual entry point.

### Non-negotiable

- Preserve user-designated unchanged regions and identity-critical anatomy, pose, silhouette, perspective, contact points, and landmark features.
- **Ordered real/abstract thesis:** make the virtual/real relationship visible at first glance. The result must contain meaningful photographic evidence and meaningful non-photographic reconstruction; neither may collapse into a full-frame filter, an almost untouched photograph, or an almost unrelated illustration. Use a few large contiguous decisions, not confetti-like alternation.
- **Living-subject unity:** keep every visible person, animal, bird, tree, or distinct connected plant anatomically and semantically coherent. Non-primary living subjects remain wholly photographic or wholly abstract. A primary person uses `face-locked, body-flexible, no-source-composite`: keep facial identity near 95% perceptual fidelity, preserve the semantic pose and contact relationship, and allow clothing, peripheral edges, color, light, material, and non-identity anatomy to be regenerated or simplified.
- **Clustered-life abstraction:** when three or more same-category living subjects are spatially close, overlap, share a common ground band, or form one repeated visual rhythm, classify them as a cluster and force the complete cluster into one coherent abstract language. Preserve only group count/range, density, direction, depth, overlap, and a few identity-carrying silhouettes. An explicitly protected primary person using `face-locked, body-flexible, no-source-composite` may remain an exception, while all surrounding cluster members remain mandatory abstraction targets.
- **Crowd single-tone hard-edge lock:** every non-primary person belonging to a crowd, queue, procession, pedestrian flow, or repeated pedestrian group must use exactly one shared opaque flat fill color across the entire image. Keep hue, value, opacity, texture, and crisp edge behavior constant. An explicitly protected primary person using `face-locked, body-flexible, no-source-composite` may remain an exception and is excluded from the crowd fill.
- **Nonliving duality:** every major architecture, engineered structure, terrain mass, mountain, cliff, shore, water body, road, or designed ground plane should visibly combine one calm photographic recognition region with one contiguous abstract reconstruction region. Aim for a restrained internal balance rather than literal equal halves: normally keep each side about 30–70% of that object's visible area. Very small, distant, or heavily occluded elements may use one coherent supporting language when a credible internal division is impossible. This rule never applies to disposable support fixtures, which default to complete abstraction.
- **Boundary discipline:** place real/abstract transitions on source-grounded seams—structural joints, façade bays, rooflines, perspective axes, shorelines, currents, ridges, slopes, shadow edges, or occlusion boundaries. Let edges stop and reappear, but do not use random blobs, uniform center splits, micro-checkerboarding, or unrelated torn shapes.
- **Vocabulary restraint:** use one primary abstract medium and at most one supporting medium across the composition. Reuse their mark density, edge behavior, and value hierarchy consistently. Contrast should come from reality versus abstraction, not from stacking many unrelated styles.
- **High-line-density forced abstraction:** inventory every architecture, vessel, vehicle, fixture, furniture element, or designed object with dense repeated lines or units—such as windows, balconies, louvers, railings, grilles, roof tiles, brick joints, deck levels, portholes, lifeboats, cables, seat rows, or paving seams. When that repetition creates high-frequency noise, interrupts a major color mass, forms an unintended visual center, or competes with the designated hero, abstraction is mandatory rather than optional. Preserve the object's location, footprint, silhouette, perspective, direction, contact role, and only the one or two line systems required for recognition. Replace the remaining repetition with 2–6 broad unequal planes, merged bands, omissions, and a sparse structural line budget. A supporting object must end with lower contrast, edge sharpness, mark density, and color variety than the hero. Blur, desaturation, texture overlay, or line-by-line tracing does not satisfy this rule.
- **Tiered abstract coverage:** materially reconstruct `light` at 30–40%, `medium` at 45–60% (default first-pass target), and `strong` at 65–85% of final visible artwork area. Use clearly non-photographic, source-semantic language such as structural linework, graphite/charcoal drawing, abstract masses, paper omission/cut-paper planes, oil-paint planes, watercolor, or ink wash. Count only areas where continuous photographic microdetail and surface structure are replaced. Global grading, desaturation, blur, haze, posterization, paper/canvas texture, grain, sharpening, or whole-frame painterly filters do not count.
- **Multi-region transformation:** distribute counted abstraction across at least two major nonliving semantic regions—for example architecture plus ground, water plus terrain, shoreline plus supporting buildings, or road plus built fixtures. A single large flat sky or background field cannot satisfy the requirement by itself. Fully photographic living subjects are exempt from abstraction but do not reduce the coverage target for the remaining composition.
- **Photographic-corridor cap:** no single continuous environmental region retaining photographic microdetail may exceed 35% of the full artwork area. Break oversized photographic corridors through semantic reconstruction, not blur, texture overlays, or arbitrary deletion.
- **Architectural presence lock:** preserve the existence, source location, footprint, silhouette role, and depth function of every visible building or substantial architectural mass. A non-hero building may be aggressively simplified, but it must remain legible through a source-derived outline, sparse structural line system, paper-cut silhouette, ink mass, or 2–5 broad planes. Never replace existing architecture with generic blur, atmospheric smearing, vegetation, sky, blank paper, or an invented opening unless the user explicitly requests removal.
- **Disposable support fixtures — zero photographic retention by default:** classify every non-hero bench, chair, stool, seat, cushion, sofa, low wall used as seating, table, or comparable furniture/support surface as disposable. Rebuild it completely in one abstract language. Integrate the person above it while preserving anatomy, weight, and contact; garment or body edges may blend into the artwork, but the fixture retains no photographic material unless explicitly protected.
- **Primary-face perceptual fidelity:** preserve approximately 95% of the primary person's recognizable facial identity by visual comparison, not raw pixel equality. This is a facial-identity target, not a 95% restoration of the whole person, body contour, garment, pose details, or source pixels. Lock face shape and proportions, eye spacing and gaze, brow, nose, mouth, expression, eyewear, hairline, ears, jaw, facial hair, and distinctive asymmetries. Small shifts in color, lighting, texture, or edge treatment are allowed when identity remains immediately recognizable and the artwork becomes more coherent.
- **Face-locked, body-flexible integration:** treat the face as the protected high-fidelity region. Preserve expression, gaze, semantic pose, overall body coherence, and meaningful contact relationships, but allow clothing, hair outside the identity-critical hairline, shoulders, arms, legs, shoes, body contour, peripheral anatomy, local light, material, and color to be regenerated, stylized, simplified, or mildly deformed. The priority order is: facial identity; expression/gaze; pose and body coherence; absence of compositing artifacts; scene integration; artistic coherence; garment/body-edge fidelity; exact source pixels.
- **No Source-Person Reinsertion:** never preserve or repair a person by cutting the original person, face, body, garment, or local photographic region out of the source image and compositing it back into the generated artwork. Do not use source compositing, source-pixel restoration, cutout reinsertion, face patching, body patching, or local source patches as identity-repair methods. Identity preservation must be achieved through generation or localized editing, not source-pixel reinsertion. If the face is insufficiently recognizable, regenerate or locally re-edit the face/person region; do not paste the original face or person back. A slightly stylized or imperfect body edge is preferable to any accidental compositing artifact.
- **Intentional boundary versus compositing failure:** an intentional thin white sticker outline, paper-cut silhouette edge, graphic contour, controlled editorial halo, or cut-paper treatment is acceptable when it is uniform, deliberate, and integrated with the visual language. Misaligned cutouts, duplicated contours, shifted person layers, double edges, partial body offsets, mismatched source patches, body/background seams caused by compositing, pasted-face artifacts, pasted-person artifacts, and floating source-photo fragments are prohibited.
- **95% identity rubric:** assess the face at matched scale and normal viewing size. Pass only when the face is immediately identifiable and no major facial identity carrier is materially wrong. Treat the percentage as an art-direction target for facial identity, not a biometric calculation or whole-person pixel target. If uncertain, compare source and final side by side and correct the smallest failing facial feature through localized generation or editing. Never restore the whole person or paste a source region to solve facial drift.
- **Visual-master priority:** the strongest artistically coherent result is the delivery candidate. Never replace a successful image with a pixelated, posterized, coarsely quantized, lower-resolution, generically masked, or separately recreated fallback in pursuit of technical identity equality.
- This living-subject rule does not apply to non-individual continuous scenery such as sky, water, terrain, mountain, shore, or a grass field treated as one ground mass; these are nonliving scene structures and should carry the controlled half-real/half-abstract relationship.
- Use generation or localized editing only, according to which method best preserves facial identity without weakening the artwork. Source compositing and deterministic source-person restoration are prohibited, including as a fallback or emergency repair. Do not require deterministic full-person restoration.
- Do not invent facial anatomy, provenance, locations, quotations, or capture dates. Preserve the hue identity of living and natural regions—skin, people, animals, sky, clouds, water, vegetation, and natural terrain. Adjust them only semantically and within a plausible neighboring range. Record deliberate color displacement of human-built regions in the palette map.
- **Protected-nature contrast routing:** diagnose the dominant natural field, hero built mass, and ground/designed mass together before selecting a palette. If their hue, value, and temperature relationships are too similar to establish a clear hierarchy, keep natural/living hue identities source-neighbor plausible and route the needed contrast through one or two human-built color levers: architecture, podium, paving, wall, bench, fixture, road, vehicle, or another designed surface. Prefer the largest identity-safe built region that can separate the major masses with one coherent shift. The hero building may change color when necessary, but preserve its silhouette, recognition-critical features, material value hierarchy, and light direction. Apply the displacement to the complete semantic region, not scattered patches, and do not recolor more built regions than needed.
- Keep each large color field within one semantic region. Do not wash across architecture, sky, ground, water, people, or another major subject.
- **Unified-sky field:** treat the entire visible sky as one continuous natural color field. Preserve one source-derived dominant hue family across the sky; allow only smooth same-family value, temperature, or atmospheric gradients and source-grounded cloud transitions. Never split an abstracted sky into multiple discrete large polygons, cut-paper blocks, ribbons, wedges, or unrelated color fields. Reserve hard-edged plane fragmentation for built structures and designed surfaces, not open sky.
- Keep exact final lettering legible. Add it deterministically when image generation cannot render it reliably.
- **Default semantic paper field:** unless the user explicitly requests full bleed, place the authored image inside a source-orientation rectangular paper sheet—3:5 portrait for portrait/square sources, 5:3 landscape for landscape sources—as one source-semantic non-rectangular content field. Keep roughly 68–82% of the available paper field active and meaningful paper negative space on at least three sides. The field must not be a softened rectangle, generic oval, vignette, automatic cutout, or arbitrary blob.
- **Content-boundary semantics:** derive one dominant outer contour and at most one quieter supporting contour from visible evidence: canopy/branch fall for foliage, roofline/façade/street perspective for architecture, ridge/slope/dune for terrain, tide/current/coast/wake for water, cloud flow for sky-led scenes, or pose/gesture/shadow/contact-ground for person-led scenes. Dry brush, ink depletion, watercolor bloom, and paper-fiber disappearance may soften this structure but cannot replace it. Alpha or a mask implements the design; edge-keying never chooses it.

### Default strategies

- Apply a natural/built color boundary. Keep people, animals, sky, clouds, water, vegetation, and natural terrain source-faithful, allowing only restrained semantic shifts in brightness, saturation, temperature, or atmospheric value. Permit stronger palette displacement on human-built architecture, paving, walls, furniture, fixtures, roads, vehicles, and designed objects when it strengthens the proposition and separates the poster from the source. Treat clothing as designed material: shift it moderately only when identity and hierarchy remain intact.
- When the dominant natural, architecture, and ground colors cluster too closely, do not accept a low-impact factual palette by default. Compare at least three candidate interventions—shift the hero architecture, shift the designed ground/fixtures, or split the adjustment across both—and choose the smallest coherent built-region change that creates clear figure/ground separation. Favor complementary temperature, useful value separation, or controlled saturation contrast; avoid arbitrary neon, equal-intensity competition, and hue changes that weaken landmark identity.
- Inventory every living subject before editing. Detect clusters first and assign each cluster one shared `fully abstract` language; then assign isolated non-primary subjects `fully photographic` or `fully abstract` and the primary person `face-locked, body-flexible, no-source-composite`. Keep facial identity, semantic pose, and biological continuity coherent while allowing controlled regeneration and transitions through clothing and peripheral body edges.
- **Auto-candidate `robot-dreams-logic` palette:** always honor an explicit user request first; otherwise evaluate this palette automatically. Mark it `candidate` when the source has weak or scattered palette hierarchy, human-built/designed regions occupy roughly 25% or more of the artwork, the scene supports restrained everyday, travel, urban, interior, seasonal, nostalgic, or quietly emotional treatment, and no documentary or color-critical constraint dominates. Compare two semantic palette plans before generation: A=`shifted`, B=`robot-dreams-logic`. Select B automatically only when it clearly improves at least two of palette hierarchy, emotional coherence, and subject/ground hierarchy without weakening identity, landmark recognition, material logic, or protected natural hues; otherwise record `fallback` and use A. Set `off` for primarily natural scenes with insufficient built color levers, already strong/coherent source palettes, documentary or historical color requirements, uniforms/rituals, brand/product color fidelity, or high-energy/neon intentions. When selected, borrow general color logic only—never a frame, character, composition, or exact sampled palette. Use 4–6 softened colors, a warm low-chroma ground, one restrained cool family, one restrained warm family, charcoal instead of pure black, and at most one concentrated emotional accent such as coral, tomato red, mustard, or clear blue. Prefer flat or semi-flat regions with 2–3 value steps. Keep skin and protected natural/living regions source-neighbor plausible and route stronger shifts through complete human-built regions.
- **Monochromatic-scene palette expansion for `robot-dreams-logic`:** before palette selection, diagnose whether the source is effectively monochromatic: one hue dominates most of the frame, warm/cool separation is weak, major semantic masses have low chromatic contrast, or a source-faithful edit would read only as minor color correction. When triggered, palette fidelity is no longer the primary objective; prefer `palette_mode: expressive` or a deliberate shifted palette. When no stronger user-specified palette reference overrides it, use `robot-dreams-logic` as the preferred cinematic color-logic reference, interpreting it rather than imitating it literally. Candidate colors may include muted peach, dusty pink, soft lilac, desaturated teal, softened blue, creamy yellow, and warm off-white highlights, arranged as restrained pastel warm/cool opposition. Do not merely intensify the dominant source hue: a blue-dominated image must gain a controlled secondary temperature family through suitable semantic regions. Route new color primarily through atmospheric gradients, reflected light, wet surfaces, haze, clouds, architectural planes, designed ground, or non-identity-critical illumination. Preserve scene structure, lighting direction, semantic boundaries, and subject identity even when color departs substantially from the source. Color displacement may be visibly stronger than the original when strict source fidelity would make the intervention insignificant, but every added hue must remain coherent with the scene's light, material, atmosphere, or reflected-color logic. Reject results where the only visible change is saturation, white balance, contrast, a global tint, or minor blue/orange grading. A successful result must produce an immediately perceptible palette transformation while preserving the scene's emotional identity and separating previously merged semantic masses. Record the diagnosis, selected palette mode, warm/cool routing, and rejected conservative alternative in `robot_dreams_auto_assessment`.
- Use scene-semantic brightness. Favor calm middle-to-lower values for quiet, historic, intimate, autumnal, or overcast scenes; allow brighter values for sunny, snowy, seaside, summer, or celebratory scenes.
- Build a few unequal semantic masses. Use an echo color only when it carries a necessary structural or conceptual turn; otherwise set it to `none`. Never add accent marks merely because a source hue is available.
- Partially deconstruct prominent tall nonliving anchors while retaining decisive silhouette, spatial role, and one or two identifying features. Make the intervention legible through one contiguous reconstructed zone, large omissions, and a few hierarchical marks; do not substitute all-over sketch texture for deconstruction.
- When one architecture or landmark is conspicuously dominant, establish it as the single hero building. Preserve its recognition-critical core and give it the clearest controlled intervention. Demote all nonessential buildings into one quiet supporting system and abstract them as far as spatial coherence allows through ink, oil-paint planes, sparse sketch, paper omission, or geometric masses.
- Fully abstract disposable support fixtures by default, even when their texture appears visually quiet or helps explain contact. Inventory every bench, chair, stool, seat, cushion, sofa, low seating wall, table, and comparable support before editing; assign the entire fixture a zero-photographic-retention treatment. Preserve contact logic through abstract geometry and a coherently generated or locally edited person above it, never through a photographic patch of the fixture or source-person reinsertion.
- Omit or strongly merge even truthful source elements when they fragment the intended semantic masses without supporting recognition or the artistic proposition. Preserve their spatial function, not necessarily their literal detail. This omission rule does not permit removing buildings or substantial architectural masses; demote them through linework, silhouettes, or broad planes instead.
- Give a dominant low-variety region contained value bands or material rhythm; keep construction-line language mainly on architecture and engineered structures.
- Run the line-density inventory before generation and again after the first pass. Treat repeated-line complexity as a hierarchy problem, not as evidence worth preserving by default. Keep a defining façade grid, shell arc, truss, funnel, deck break, or directional seam only when it carries recognition; merge all subordinate repetitions into their semantic parent plane.
- Reduce clustered pedestrians and nonessential secondary pedestrians to one coherent single-tone silhouette system. Use exactly one shared opaque flat fill across all such people in the image; preserve only group range, density, proportion, pose rhythm, direction, spacing, and necessary overlap. Use crisp hard edges, merge collisions into clean union masses or open a narrow paper-colored separation gap, and allow no face, garment detail, tonal depth steps, transparency, hatch, paper grain, sketch texture, ink/watercolor wash, photographic islands, internal modeling, or edge bleed.
- Keep hierarchy classes visually distinct. Do not give secondary people the structural line language used for architecture. Assign photographic texture to identity anchors, sparse structural marks to selected built forms, and solid fill-only silhouettes to crowds.

### Auto-active architectural module and optional modules

- **Cross-cultural architectural logic:** default `auto-active` and mandatory to assess in every architecture-led scene. This is a spatial and structural operation, never a painting-style swap. For Western or Western-modern hero architecture, run a `huizhou-spatial-trial` on one contiguous secondary zone covering roughly 20–35% of the visible building. Preserve the overall massing, proportions, decisive silhouette, structural identity, perspective, joints, load logic, light direction, and recognizable identity of the Western building. Do not fully transform it into a traditional Chinese building. Instead, selectively orientalize parts of the architecture—especially the roof, façade, entrance, and transitional spaces—using Huizhou and Jiangnan elements such as white plaster walls, dark grey tiled roofs, horse-head walls, wooden lattice screens, deep overhanging eaves, covered corridors, and quiet courtyards. The building must remain fundamentally Western in structure while being partially infused with the elegant, restrained, poetic character of Jiangnan architecture. Aim for a serene, refined, misty, scholarly atmosphere. Use layered enclosure, framed views, alternating solid/void, stepped wall depth, reveal/conceal sequence, compressed-to-open spatial rhythm, and asymmetric roof/wall cadence to make the intervention spatially legible. Integrate these elements selectively within a source-grounded zone rather than applying them as a full-building costume; do not invent unsupported openings, courtyards, or historical provenance. For Eastern, especially Huizhou or Jiangnan, hero architecture, run a `reciprocal-modernist-trial` on one contiguous secondary zone covering roughly 20–35% of the visible building. Preserve the main body and spatial character of the Eastern building, including white walls, dark grey tiled roofs, horse-head walls, courtyards, covered corridors, and its quiet Jiangnan identity. Do not fully convert it into a Western building. Instead, selectively westernize the entrance, openings, façade order, and selected architectural details through arches, columns, pediments, pilasters, or a restrained modern Western façade language. The result should read as an Eastern architectural body subtly invaded by Western order, monumentality, and detail, while retaining the quiet, poetic, restrained atmosphere of Jiangnan architecture. Preserve source-grounded massing, perspective, joints, load logic, light direction, and recognition core; do not invent unsupported historical provenance. When cultural classification is uncertain, use a `geometry-only-trial`: select whichever spatial logic best clarifies the source geometry without naming or implying provenance. Disable a trial only when it demonstrably weakens recognition, structure, or hierarchy; otherwise retain the smallest successful intervention.
- **Cloud metaphor:** default `off`. Enable only when the user requests it or an existing cloud formation makes the metaphor unusually natural. Never add clouds merely to create a motif.
- **Cinematic title:** default `off`. Enable when requested or when a released-film connection is exceptionally strong and verifiable; otherwise use user wording or a source-grounded original title.
- **Illustrated/anime portrait:** explicit-only. Never select it solely because a face is front-facing. Enable only when the user requests illustration/anime treatment or clearly accepts identity reinterpretation.

## Abstraction Strength

- **`light` — 30–40%:** retain most identity/documentary evidence, but reconstruct at least two identity-safe semantic regions. Prefer for sensitive portraits, complex contact points, or fragile landmarks.
- **`medium` — 45–60% (default):** retain selected photographic anchors while replacing substantial portions of at least two major regions with semantic masses, drawing, paint, ink, paper omission, or partial landmark reconstruction. The first generated pass must target this range, not the 30% floor.
- **`strong` — 65–85%:** rely on large masses, paper omissions, broken planes, displaced axes, drawing/paint systems, and sparse photographic anchors. Use for robust silhouettes, scenery, distant figures, or explicit requests.

## Person Modes

- **`face-locked, body-flexible, no-source-composite`:** default for a primary person. Preserve approximately 95% perceptual facial identity, expression, gaze, hairline, and jaw; preserve pose and contact semantically; allow clothing, peripheral hair, body contour, peripheral anatomy, local lighting, texture, and silhouette transitions to be regenerated or integrated. Never paste source pixels back.
- **`fully-abstract-faceless`:** use only for distant, blurred, shadowed, obscured, or nonessential people. Rebuild the whole visible person in one coherent graphic language while preserving direction, posture, silhouette, clothing mass, and light; retain no photographic islands and invent no facial features.
- **`illustrated-portrait`:** this is the only mode allowed to unlock and redraw a primary face. Use only after explicit user intent for illustration/anime treatment and accepted identity reinterpretation. Preserve source-specific proportions, pose, garments, hands, light, and recognizable cues; avoid generic character design.
- Select among these modes from subject importance, identity sensitivity, face clarity, requested intensity, and user intent. Back-facing direction alone does not authorize identity reinterpretation.
- In face-locked mode, retain natural skin character and source-specific facial proportions while allowing restrained tonal and material integration. Avoid invented facial anatomy, plastic smoothing, generic facial replacement, or inconsistent rendering across facial features. The body may be simplified or regenerated when this improves visual coherence.
- Inspect the face at matched scale and normal viewing size. Correct drift in the smallest relevant identity carrier—eye/gaze, nose, mouth/expression, eyewear, hairline, jaw, or face proportion—without automatically restoring the whole person.
- Avoid accidental separation. Let garment and body edges participate in the composition through controlled blending, recoloring, texture, reconstruction, or an intentional thin sticker/paper-cut/graphic contour. A deliberate stylized edge is acceptable; a compositing artifact is never acceptable.

## Editorial Copy and Date

1. Use exact user-provided wording whenever the user supplies fixed text.
2. Otherwise derive one concise title from visible subject relationships, light, movement, distance, time-of-day impression, structural axis, spatial tension, or a meaningful source-color relationship. Prefer a restrained metaphor grounded in visible evidence. Avoid generic words such as `Memory`, `Dream`, or `Moment` unless specifically justified; avoid travel-advertising language, camera terminology, grand narratives, and arbitrary location labels.
3. Default to one main title only: 2–5 English words, concise, poetic but source-grounded, visually small, and never an advertising headline. Use `copy_mode: poetic` or `editorial` as appropriate; use `cinematic` only when its optional module is enabled; use `none` when words weaken the composition.
4. Add a subtitle only when it contributes a genuinely different semantic layer. Keep it to 3–7 English words, smaller and lighter than the title, preferably in an elegant italic serif. Never add a subtitle merely to fill empty space. In the default `processed` orientation-adaptive editorial collage, the top white date band may carry a restrained date by default, using `DateTimeOriginal`, a reliable embedded creation/digitization date, or the poster-generation date when capture metadata is absent. Outside that banded collage mode, do not force a date, serial number, archive label, location label, or metadata line unless explicitly requested.
5. If the user requests film-title matching or the optional cinematic-title module is justified, select only a semantically fitting released film and verify its exact title and release status. Do not imply that the photo depicts the film, actors, or filming location.
6. Use an `elegant restrained editorial serif` by default. For architecture, city, and structural scenes use a composed book serif with stable proportions and restrained contrast. For nature, light, and lyrical scenes use a fine humanist serif with lighter visual weight. Do not default to bold commercial sans serif, advertising typography, cartoon fonts, exaggerated handwriting, decorative display fonts, or oversized all-caps.
7. Choose typography color from a dark restrained hue related to the artwork—deep blue-gray, charcoal gray, dark green, wine red, deep muted purple, or dark brown-gray. Do not default to pure black, and never use the brightest accent color unless the composition specifically requires it. Subtitle color should remain in the same hue family at a lighter value or lower saturation.
8. Treat typography as part of the composition from the beginning. First reserve and protect a genuine typography zone during generation; do not search for leftover emptiness after the artwork is complete. Keep sufficient breathing room around the text and do not squeeze it between existing objects.
9. Before placement, diagnose dominant visual weight, abstract-mass position, primary-subject position, major axis, quiet negative-space zones, and the eye-path exit point. Choose one of these layouts:
   - **Lower-left editorial alignment:** use when the subject or visual weight is right-biased, the composition extends horizontally, and stable lower-left space exists. Keep the text approximately 6–9% from the left and bottom edges.
   - **Bottom-centered editorial alignment:** use when the subject is near center, the building or composition has a clear central axis, the visual relation is relatively symmetrical, and stable lower space exists. Align the title with the visual axis without placing it close to the subject or abstract motif.
   - **Source-aware quiet zone:** use another position only when it has explicit support from the visual weight, axis, motif position, or eye-path exit. Never choose a corner solely because it has the lowest edge density.
10. Keep typography clearly subordinate. At normal viewing size it must be legible but must not become the first visual entry point. Do not enlarge the title merely because the quiet area is large. Use no decorative rules, icons, fake metadata, high-contrast headline blocks, or multiple competing font families.

## Output Modes

### Orientation-Adaptive Editorial Collage

The default `processed` output uses one consistent banded editorial collage system for both landscape and portrait sources, but its proportions and abstract geometry must adapt to source orientation. The system is:

```text
[ top white date band ]
[ faithful photo section ]
[ black band for main title ]
[ lower abstract section ]
[ black band for subtitle ]
```

The information hierarchy never changes: the top white band carries the date, the first black band carries the main title, and the final black band carries the optional subtitle. The source orientation changes the section proportions, photographic crop strategy, abstract-motif direction, and negative-space relationship.

- **Landscape source:** use the landscape profile; keep the upper photographic region wider and shallower, permit a more horizontally extended abstract motif, and preserve horizontal visual movement.
- **Portrait source:** use the portrait profile; make the photographic region visibly taller, preserve the portrait reading direction, avoid forcing the image into a shallow horizontal strip, and organize the lower abstraction vertically, centrally, or along a strong vertical axis. Preserve necessary headroom and footroom around tall subjects.
- **Near-square source:** use the square profile; balance the photo and abstract sections without mechanically choosing the landscape profile, and let the abstract axis follow the source's strongest vertical, horizontal, or central relationship.

For portrait sources, prioritize source integrity over template regularity. Mild cropping is allowed, but do not stretch, compress, severely horizontal-crop, or naturally truncate a person, building, tower, tree, doorway, column, mountain, or other tall subject merely to preserve landscape proportions. The lower abstract motif must retain vertical axis, height hierarchy, upward extension, centerline or offset-axis logic, and the source's subject-to-blank-space relationship. It may be more concentrated and taller, but must remain a free, non-rectangular, boundaryless form rather than a small bottom icon or a low horizontal strip.

Reserve the typography bands and the lower abstract negative space during generation. Keep the black bands restrained, especially for portrait sources: the taller photo section carries the primary visual weight, while the lower abstraction and subtitle remain quieter so the whole page retains an upper-heavy editorial balance.

**Orientation-Adaptive Editorial Collage is non-negotiable:** keep one consistent editorial collage system, but adapt proportions and abstract geometry to the source orientation. Portrait images must remain portrait-led compositions, not flattened landscape templates.

- Offer three selectable modes:
  - **`processed` — default orientation-adaptive editorial collage:** output a rectangular paper sheet using the banded editorial collage system. Keep the faithful source photo in the upper photo section, place the main title in the first black band, place the source-derived abstract reconstruction in the lower section, and place the optional subtitle in the final black band. The lower abstract area must remain source-semantic and free-edged rather than a generic rectangle; do not include a separate original-comparison panel.
  - **`fullbleed` — edge-to-edge treated image:** output only the generated treatment as one complete rectangular image in its source-compatible orientation, with no paper field or comparison layout.
  - **`postcard` — original/generated postcard:** output the vertical comparison layout described below.
- When the user explicitly asks for a postcard, comparison, original plus generated image, or reference-style layout, use `postcard`.
- When the user asks for a processed image, poster, single image, or standalone result, use default `processed` orientation-adaptive editorial collage mode. Use `fullbleed` only when the user explicitly asks for edge-to-edge, no paper margin, or a complete rectangular treatment.
- When the user does not specify a mode, default to `processed`. Offer or additionally provide `postcard` when comparison is useful, but do not replace the requested standalone image with a postcard.

## Postcard Layout

- Output a vertical 3:5 postcard by default, not a square 1:1 comparison.
- Derive the postcard ground from the source image's mood and palette: warm cream, cool paper gray, dusty blue, muted sage, pale sand, or another quiet source-adjacent tint. Keep enough contrast for both images and type.
- Draw one restrained rectangular keyline around the overall inner framework and a separate rectangular frame around the upper original photograph.
- Place the unchanged original photograph in the upper zone as the clearest factual reference, but cap it at 45% of the inner framework's usable area and roughly 32–40% of inner height. Do not enlarge it merely to fill width.
- Give the lower interpretation field—generated artwork plus selected copy—at least 50% of the inner framework's usable area. Divide it according to the source's visual weight, axis, abstract-mass position, and protected quiet zone; the generated interpretation must function as a co-primary authored result rather than a thumbnail.
- Treat postcard copy as a small editorial annotation, never a competing panel. Prefer lower-left or bottom-centered alignment according to the typography rules, and use another source-aware quiet zone only with explicit compositional justification. Keep it subordinate, preserve breathing room, and reduce or omit optional levels before enlarging the block.
- **Semantic artwork boundary:** derive the lower artwork's visible contour from the scene's dominant spatial meaning, not from generic border removal. Use source-grounded geometry such as a coast/current/tide arc for sea or river scenes, a slope/ridgeline for terrain, a canopy contour for forest, a structural section/roofline for architecture, or another clearly evidenced directional boundary. The contour may combine one primary semantic edge with restrained dry-brush, torn-paper, erased, or feathered variation, but it must remain traceable to the source composition.
- Do not draw or imply a rectangular border around the generated artwork. Do not treat automatic alpha/edge-key removal as sufficient art direction: first design or generate the semantic contour, then use alpha, edge-key, or an explicit mask only to implement it. A generic corner-key cutout, arbitrary blob, color mismatch, shadow, glow, blur, vignette, uniform rectangular fade, or accidental missing patch counts as an unwanted frame/boundary.
- Preserve ample margins and unequal visual weight. The lower generated image may be larger than the copy block but must remain subordinate to the upper original photograph.

## Identity Review

For every visible primary face, inspect the final artwork beside the source at matched face scale and at normal viewing size. Use this order:

1. Confirm immediate recognizability.
2. Compare face shape/proportions, gaze and eye spacing, brows, nose, mouth and expression, eyewear, hairline, ears, jaw, facial hair, and distinctive asymmetries.
3. Require no major identity carrier to be materially wrong. Treat approximately 95% fidelity as a perceptual art-direction target, not a biometric calculation or pixel-difference threshold.
4. Permit neighboring changes in skin color, lighting, texture, edge softness, and material treatment when they support the final palette and do not alter identity.
5. Permit broader transformation of clothing, garment edges, fabric texture, peripheral hair, body-edge transitions, and local silhouette details when anatomy, gesture, and contact remain credible.
6. Prefer localized generation or editing when one facial feature drifts. Do not use source blending, face patches, body patches, or source-person reinsertion. Regenerate the person region or the complete image if necessary.

The bundled `verify_identity_lock.py` is not an identity-repair method. Do not use it to justify exact pixel preservation, source-person reinsertion, or compositing; facial recognizability and visual coherence take priority over exact body pixels.

### Person Conflict Priority

When person-related requirements conflict, resolve them in this order:

1. facial identity;
2. expression and gaze;
3. anatomical and visual coherence;
4. absence of compositing artifacts;
5. scene integration;
6. artistic coherence;
7. garment and body-detail fidelity;
8. exact source-pixel fidelity.

Exact source-pixel fidelity is always the lowest priority. Preserve the face, regenerate the person, and never paste the person back.

Inspect metadata:

```bash
python scripts/inspect_photo.py input.jpg
```

Compose final files:

```bash
python scripts/compose_poster.py \

  --original input.jpg \
  --poster edited.png \
  --title "Exact title" \
  --subtitle "Short verified quote or semantic line" \
  --date 2026.08.12 \

```

Pass `--font /path/to/font.ttf` when automatic cross-platform CJK font discovery cannot find a suitable font. The script writes a vertical 3:5 postcard by default. Use `--background-color` only when the automatically derived source-adjacent ground needs an explicit override.

Select only the required copy levels: omit `--subtitle`, pass `--no-date`, or omit both `--title` and `--subtitle` with `--no-date` for a copy-free result. Long Chinese or Latin titles wrap automatically up to `--max-title-lines 3` by default.

For the lower postcard artwork, use the safest available background mode:

- `--background-removal alpha` (default): use an existing alpha channel; keep an RGB image rectangular rather than guessing.
- `--background-removal edge-key`: remove only corner-like paper connected to the image edge. Add `--mask-preview` to inspect the derived mask.
- `--background-removal mask --art-mask mask.png`: use an explicit white-foreground/black-background mask.
- `--background-removal none`: preserve the complete rectangular artwork.

Use `fullbleed` when the poster itself must define the exact rectangular canvas.

## Quality Gate

- **`S01 proposition-drift`:** every major intervention supports the source-grounded artistic proposition; no decorative motif competes with it.
- **`R01 recognition-loss`:** the source remains recognizable through large relationships and preserved evidence rather than copied microdetail.
- **`A01 insufficient-abstraction`:** counted reconstructed area meets the chosen tier—light 30–40%, medium 45–60%, strong 65–85%—using conservative area estimates that exclude filter-like tonal/texture changes.
- **`A02 single-region-abstraction`:** counted abstraction spans at least two major semantic regions and is not satisfied only by a large sky/background field.
- **`A03 photographic-corridor`:** no continuous environmental photographic region exceeds 35% of full artwork area.
- **`C01 mass-fragmentation`:** the scene reads as a few coherent semantic masses.
- **`C02 boundary-crossing`:** color fields remain inside their semantic regions.
- **`C04 sky-fragmentation`:** the sky reads as one continuous source-derived hue field with only smooth same-family atmospheric variation; no discrete large color blocks, hard wedges, or cut-paper segmentation divide it.
- **`C03 accent-scatter`:** the source-derived echo color is concentrated, visible, and subordinate.
- **`C05 weak-palette-separation`:** when dominant natural, hero-built, and ground/designed masses begin with weak color separation, the result uses one or two coherent built-region color interventions to create a clearly stronger hue, value, or temperature hierarchy while keeping protected natural/living hues plausible.
- **`L01 landmark-generic`:** tall anchors show deliberate partial reconstruction while retaining decisive identifiers.
- **`L02 hierarchy-conflict`:** one dominant hero building remains unmistakable; supporting architecture is materially quieter, simpler, lower-contrast, and does not compete through literal detail or a second incompatible style.
- **`L03 architecture-erasure`:** every source building or substantial architectural mass remains present at its original location and depth as a simplified outline, structural line system, silhouette, ink mass, or broad-plane reconstruction; none is replaced by blur, smearing, blankness, vegetation, sky, or a false opening.
- **`P01 identity-drift`:** the person mode follows identity sensitivity and user intent; anatomy, skin, face, pose, and contact evidence remain coherent where required.
- **`P00 identity-fidelity-loss` (blocking):** the primary face is not immediately recognizable, or a major identity carrier—face proportion, gaze/eye spacing, brow, nose, mouth/expression, eyewear, hairline, ears, jaw, facial hair, or distinctive asymmetry—is materially wrong. Correct the smallest failing feature and reassess. Exact pixels, identical color, and unchanged facial texture are not required.
- **`Q00 artistic-quality-regression` (blocking):** a technically safer or more source-faithful revision is materially worse as an artwork through pixelation, posterization, coarse quantization, generic masking, pasted-person edges, lower resolution, weakened palette, or lost semantic structure. Keep the artistically stronger version and solve only the specific identity issue.
- **`P02 identity-integration-failure`:** the person appears accidentally pasted on, haloed, rectangularly patched, mismatched in sharpness/grain/light, anatomically broken, or incoherently half-rendered. A deliberate, uniform sticker outline, paper-cut edge, graphic contour, or controlled editorial halo is allowed; accidental seams, duplicated edges, shifted layers, partial body offsets, mismatched source patches, pasted-face artifacts, pasted-body artifacts, and mixed-resolution human fragments are blocking failures. Regenerate the person region or complete image; never repair by source compositing.
- **`P03 source-person-reinsertion` (blocking):** any original person, face, body, garment, or local photographic region has been cut out and pasted back into the generated artwork, whether as a default composite, identity patch, body patch, source-pixel restoration, or emergency repair. Facial identity must be preserved through generation or localized editing. Exact source-pixel fidelity is always lower priority than facial identity, anatomical/visual coherence, absence of compositing artifacts, scene integration, and artistic coherence.
- **`B01 biological-language-split`:** every non-human living individual or connected organism uses one coherent treatment across its complete visible body. No tree mixes photographic trunk/branches with abstract foliage; no animal or bird mixes photographic anatomy with graphic body regions. Reclassify the whole organism as photographic or abstract and restore/rebuild it consistently before delivery.
- **`B02 living-subject-split`:** non-primary living subjects must remain wholly photographic or wholly abstract. For the primary person, fail only when anatomy or facial identity becomes incoherent; controlled integration across clothing, peripheral edges, color, light, and material is allowed and encouraged when it strengthens the artwork.
- **`B03 living-cluster-retention`:** every detected flock, herd, crowd, queue, procession, pedestrian flow, or dense repeated plant group is fully abstracted in one shared language. No cluster member retains photographic texture or becomes an isolated detailed miniature unless it is an explicitly protected primary subject. For human groups, every non-primary person must share one identical opaque flat fill color.
- **`V01 weak-virtual-real-contrast`:** both photographic evidence and authored abstraction are materially visible; the result is neither an almost unchanged photograph nor a nearly unrelated full illustration.
- **`V02 nonliving-duality-failure`:** every major architecture and nonliving landscape region contains both a calm photographic recognition core and one contiguous abstract reconstruction zone, normally with neither side below about 30% unless scale or occlusion makes division impossible.
- **`V03 transition-disorder`:** real/abstract boundaries follow structural or landscape evidence and use a restrained shared vocabulary; no arbitrary blobs, tiny alternating patches, or competing media systems scatter the composition.
- **`P04 human-layer-offset` (blocking):** reject any duplicated person edge, shifted silhouette, partial body offset, non-overlapping source and generated body section, visible cutout seam, compositing offset, floating source-photo fragment, pasted face, pasted body, or mismatched resolution/noise/exposure in the human figure. Slight clothing-edge deformation, body-edge simplification, painterly body transition, white sticker outline, graphic contour, and paper-cut boundary are allowed only when deliberate and visually coherent. If this gate fails, regenerate the person region or the complete image; do not apply additional source compositing.
- **`H01 vocabulary-collision`:** architecture, identity anchors, and secondary people use distinct visual vocabularies; crowd silhouettes contain no architectural line work.
- **`H02 crowd-fill-contamination` (blocking):** all non-primary crowd members use one identical opaque flat color with crisp hard boundaries. Any second crowd tone, depth-value step, per-person color, gradient, transparency, internal texture or mark, sketch/graphite/ink-wash/watercolor treatment, feathered edge, paint bleed, or blended overlap blocks delivery. Correct overlaps by a clean silhouette union or a narrow unpainted/paper-colored separation gap; never by translucent stacking or soft blending.
- **`D01 detail-clutter`:** nonessential fixtures and repetitive textures are simplified.
- **`D02 disposable-support-retention` (blocking):** every non-hero bench, chair, stool, seat, cushion, sofa, low seating wall, table, or comparable support is fully abstract across its complete visible extent. Any retained photographic pixel, texture, seam, repeated unit, upholstery, masonry grain, or wear belonging to the fixture blocks delivery, including at the body-contact zone. Preserve support logic only through abstract silhouette, load-bearing edge, perspective, and a coherently generated or locally edited person interacting with the fixture. Permit photographic retention only when the user explicitly designates the fixture itself as a subject, landmark, product, artwork, or identity-bearing object.
- **`D03 high-line-density-retention`:** no complex built object or designed surface retains enough repeated linework or units to create high-frequency noise, fragment a major mass, or compete with the hero; triggered regions are structurally compressed into 2–6 broad unequal planes with only recognition-critical lines remaining.
- **`M01 unjustified-module`:** optional modules appear only when enabled and propositionally relevant.
- **`M02 palette-reference-overreach`:** a named palette reference contributes only general color relationships; it does not copy exact frames, characters, compositions, or force natural/living regions outside plausible source-neighbor hues.
- **`M03 cross-cultural-architecture-overreach`:** the trial uses spatial/structural principles rather than a painting-style overlay, stays within one contiguous 20–35% secondary architectural zone on first pass, and preserves overall massing, proportions, silhouette, structural identity, perspective, joints, load logic, light, recognition core, and hierarchy. A Western-building Huizhou/Jiangnan trial may selectively introduce white plaster walls, dark grey tiled roofs, horse-head walls, wooden lattice screens, deep overhanging eaves, covered corridors, quiet courtyards, layered enclosure, framed views, solid/void rhythm, stepped depth, reveal/conceal sequence, compressed/open rhythm, and roof/wall cadence. It must remain fundamentally Western in structure and must not become a fully traditional Chinese building or imply unsupported historical provenance. An Eastern-building reciprocal-modernist trial must preserve the Huizhou/Jiangnan body and atmosphere while selectively introducing Western arches, columns, pediments, pilasters, façade order, monumentality, or restrained modern Western detail. It must not become a fully Western building or erase white walls, dark grey tiled roofs, horse-head walls, courtyards, covered corridors, or the quiet poetic character of Jiangnan architecture. Reject either intervention if it becomes costume-like, over-literal, structurally implausible, historically misleading, or weakens recognition.
- **`M05 cross-cultural-architecture-omission` (blocking):** every architecture-led scene records and performs the cross-cultural architectural-logic assessment. Western or Western-modern hero architecture receives a Huizhou spatial trial by default; East Asian traditional hero architecture may receive a reciprocal modernist trial; uncertain architecture receives a geometry-only trial. Skipping the assessment or returning directly to neutral media without a documented recognition/structure/hierarchy failure blocks delivery.
- **`M04 robot-dreams-auto-misfire`:** an automatically selected `robot-dreams-logic` palette has sufficient built/design color levers, wins at least two of three comparisons against ordinary `shifted` color—palette hierarchy, emotional coherence, subject/ground hierarchy—and preserves documentary truth, identity, landmark recognition, material logic, and protected natural hues; for effectively monochromatic scenes it must also create an immediately perceptible, semantically routed warm/cool expansion rather than only apply saturation, white-balance, contrast, global-tint, or minor blue/orange grading. Every added hue must remain coherent with light, material, atmosphere, or reflected-color logic; otherwise it is off or falls back.
- **`T01 copy-generic`:** original copy is specific to the source and proposition; quotations and cinematic references are verified.
- **`T02 typography-failure`:** selected text levels are exact, legible, refined, box-free, subordinate, source-aware, and placed within a protected negative-space zone without covering critical evidence. Reject or reposition typography when it becomes a focal point, overlaps a face or primary subject, sits on dense detail, uses arbitrary corner placement, defaults to unjustified pure black, behaves like advertising, or appears pasted on after the artwork is complete.
- **`O01 wrong-mode`:** processed paper-field, fullbleed, and postcard deliveries follow their respective composition rules.
- **`O03 semantic-boundary-failure`:** the lower postcard artwork has a source-grounded contour expressing the scene's dominant geometry—such as coast/current, slope, canopy, or architecture—rather than a rectangle, generic cutout, arbitrary blob, or automatic edge-key artifact.
- **`O04 postcard-proportion-failure`:** the original photo occupies no more than 45% of the inner usable area and roughly 32–40% of inner height; the lower generated-artwork/copy field occupies at least 50%, and the generated artwork is not reduced to a decorative thumbnail.
- **`O05 postcard-copy-dominance`:** postcard copy uses lower-left, bottom-centered, or another explicitly justified source-aware quiet-zone placement; it remains subordinate to both image fields, does not become a primary focal point, and is not selected merely by lowest edge density.
- **`O06 semantic-content-field-failure`:** default processed output is a rectangular paper sheet with one source-semantic non-rectangular content field occupying roughly 68–82% of paper area, meaningful negative space on at least three sides, and no generic oval, rounded rectangle, blob, vignette, or automatic edge-key contour.
- **`O07 orientation-adaptive-collage-failure` (blocking):** the default processed output must use the same five-part editorial collage hierarchy—top white date band, faithful photo section, black main-title band, lower abstract section, black subtitle band—while adapting proportions to source orientation. Landscape sources use the landscape profile and a wider, shallower photo region with horizontally extended abstraction; portrait sources use the portrait profile, a visibly taller photo region, source-integrity-first cropping, and a vertically organized or concentrated lower motif; near-square sources use the square profile. Reject portrait outputs that are flattened into shallow horizontal strips, aggressively horizontally cropped, unnaturally truncate tall subjects, or reuse a low horizontal abstract template. Reject any output that sacrifices source orientation logic merely to preserve fixed bands.
- **`O08 band-role-inconsistency`:** the top white band carries the date, the first black band carries the main title, and the final black band carries the optional subtitle. Band roles may not swap between landscape, portrait, and near-square sources. Black bands must remain restrained and subordinate, especially for portrait sources.
- **`O02 filter-look`:** the first pass visibly changes at least two of these three properties in each counted region—outer/inner structure, material/rendering language, or region boundary/omission pattern—and reads as authored reconstruction rather than global styling. If it remains plausible as the original photo with altered color/texture, fail immediately and regenerate at the chosen tier.

Record only the failed codes. Correct one code at a time, preserve successful regions, then re-check the full gate.
