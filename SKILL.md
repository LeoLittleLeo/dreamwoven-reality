# Interweave Photo Abstraction

> **License notice:** This skill is licensed for personal, non-commercial use only. Whenever the skill, a modified version, or work materially created with it is published or shared, credit **LeoLittleLeo** and link to the [original GitHub repository](https://github.com/LeoLittleLeo/interweave-photo-abstraction). Commercial use is prohibited. See [LICENSE](LICENSE) for the complete terms.

Create an authored poster whose central aesthetic is **ordered interweaving of reality and abstraction**. Contrast comes from photographic and non-photographic regions entering, stopping, and reappearing across the scene. Order comes from a limited palette, disciplined boundaries, restrained media, and object-level consistency: every living subject is wholly photographic or wholly abstract, while major nonliving architecture and natural scenery deliberately contain both photographic evidence and abstract reconstruction.

## Workflow

1. Inspect the source and metadata. Diagnose the scene before choosing a style: scene type, primary subject, identity sensitivity, face clarity, tall anchors, dominant regions, palette, negative space, and reliable capture date.
2. Read [references/visual-direction.md](references/visual-direction.md). When the scene contains a living cluster, an architecture-led framed view, disposable interior furniture, or a dominant botanical group, also read [references/exemplars.md](references/exemplars.md) and inspect only the routed exemplar image(s). Use exemplars as structural and quality references, never as fixed palettes or composition templates. Then state one source-grounded artistic proposition: a relationship, tension, transition, or emotional fact that the poster will clarify. Do not invent a story unsupported by the image.
3. Write a compact strategy record using the schema below. Make preservation, omission, reconstruction, echo color, negative space, and copy all serve the proposition and remain traceable to visible evidence or an explicit user request.
4. Choose `light`, `medium`, or `strong` abstraction. Respect a user-requested intensity; otherwise use `medium` with a 45–60% reconstructed-area target. Lower only for identity-sensitive documentary portraits; raise for robust silhouettes, scenery, distant figures, or explicit permission.
5. Assign each visible living subject one coherent treatment. Use `face-locked, body-flexible, no-source-composite` for the primary person by default and `fully abstract` only for distant/nonessential faceless people or explicit identity reinterpretation. Keep facial identity and semantic pose coherent while allowing the body, clothing, peripheral hair, and edges to be regenerated or integrated.
6. Assign every major nonliving built or landscape region a controlled real/abstract division. Preserve a calm photographic recognition core and reconstruct a distinct contiguous portion; avoid tiny alternating patches. Plan 3–6 large semantic masses, a limited shared palette, one concentrated echo color at most, and only justified optional modules. For the lower panel, switch from division to extreme semantic reduction: retain only broad color planes, sparse structural lines, simplified silhouettes, and the minimum recognition anchors.
7. Generate or edit the complete artwork as one integrated composition. Preserve the primary person's facial identity at approximately 95% perceptual fidelity: facial geometry, expression, gaze, eyewear, hairline, jaw, and distinctive features must remain immediately recognizable. This target applies primarily to the face, not to whole-person pixels or silhouette. Exact source pixels are not required and must never be restored by source-person compositing.
8. Let final artistic coherence take priority outside core facial identity. Permit restrained changes to garment color, garment edges, lighting, texture, peripheral hair, body contour, and local silhouette transitions when they improve palette integration, material continuity, or the real/abstract thesis. Preserve pose, anatomy, gesture, and meaningful contact relationships.
9. Compare the candidate final side by side with the source at matched face scale and against the coded quality gate. Judge facial fidelity perceptually using the identity rubric; judge the complete image by composition, abstraction, boundary design, palette, finish, and absence of accidental compositing seams. If the person region fails, regenerate or locally re-edit it; never repair it by pasting source pixels back.
10. Before generation, assign every visible object to exactly one treatment using the Object Treatment Lock below. Do not begin generation while secondary people, seating fixtures, and ground planes remain unassigned.
11. Generate two coordinated interpretations from the same source: an upper photographic-led partial abstraction and a lower strong abstraction. Use `scripts/inspect_photo.py` for metadata when useful. Use `scripts/compose_poster.py` in default `processed` mode to assemble exactly those two generated stages, or explicit `fullbleed` mode for one edge-to-edge interpretation. The upper stage must be supplied as a free-edged authored image and the lower stage as a complete rectangular image. Never place the untouched source in the final composition.

## Rule Priority

Typography is subordinate to the primary subject, real/abstract composition, major color masses, and negative-space structure. It completes the layout and guides the eye toward its exit point; it must never become the primary visual entry point.

### Object Treatment Lock

Before generation, inventory the image from foreground to background and left to right. Assign every visible object to one treatment and preserve the assignment in both generated stages:

```yaml
primary_person: face-locked-body-flexible-no-source-composite
secondary_people: one-shared-opaque-flat-silhouette
animals_birds: fully-photographic | fully-abstract-per-organism
architecture: 70-percent-photographic-plus-20-35-percent-colored-architectural-sketch
seating_fixtures: fully-abstract-zero-photographic-retention
ground_pavement_platform: photographic-core-plus-contiguous-abstract-zone
sky_water_terrain: source-faithful-with-controlled-semantic-abstraction
```

Only the explicitly designated primary person may remain photographic among human figures. Every other visible person is a secondary person by default, including people at restaurants, on promenades, in queues, on benches, or dispersed across a public space. Do not preserve a secondary face, clothing texture, individual lighting, or photographic island.

Every non-hero bench, chair, cushion, sofa, seat, low seating wall, table, and comparable support is fully abstract across its complete connected extent. Remove photographic upholstery, leather, wood grain, seams, masonry texture, wear, and repeated surface detail. Preserve only abstract silhouette, load-bearing geometry, perspective, and contact logic with the person.

Every visible ground plane, pavement, promenade, road, platform, or terrace must contain at least one clearly contiguous abstract reconstruction zone. Preserve its perspective, slope, edge, and spatial function, but replace repeated paving detail and surface microtexture with broad semantic planes, sparse perspective lines, controlled omissions, or one restrained colored/graphic medium. Color grading, blur, grain, texture overlay, and painterly filtering alone do not satisfy this requirement.

If any object assignment is not visibly executed in the candidate, the candidate fails and must be locally regenerated or re-edited before delivery. Do not treat a photographic-looking secondary person, seat, or ground plane as an acceptable fallback for scene readability.

### Non-negotiable

- Preserve user-designated unchanged regions and identity-critical anatomy, pose, silhouette, perspective, contact points, and landmark features.
- **Ordered real/abstract thesis:** make the virtual/real relationship visible at first glance. The result must contain meaningful photographic evidence and meaningful non-photographic reconstruction; neither may collapse into a full-frame filter, an almost untouched photograph, or an almost unrelated illustration. Use a few large contiguous decisions, not confetti-like alternation.
- **Living-subject unity:** keep every visible person, animal, bird, tree, or distinct connected plant anatomically and semantically coherent. Non-primary living subjects remain wholly photographic or wholly abstract. A primary person uses `face-locked, body-flexible, no-source-composite`: keep facial identity near 95% perceptual fidelity, preserve the semantic pose and contact relationship, and allow clothing, peripheral edges, color, light, material, and non-identity anatomy to be regenerated or simplified.
- **Clustered-life abstraction:** when three or more same-category living subjects are spatially close, overlap, share a common ground band, or form one repeated visual rhythm, classify them as a cluster and force the complete cluster into one coherent abstract language. Preserve only group count/range, density, direction, depth, overlap, and a few identity-carrying silhouettes. An explicitly protected primary person using `face-locked, body-flexible, no-source-composite` may remain an exception, while all surrounding cluster members remain mandatory abstraction targets.
- **Crowd single-tone hard-edge lock:** every non-primary person belonging to a crowd, queue, procession, pedestrian flow, or repeated pedestrian group must use exactly one shared opaque flat fill color across the entire image. Keep hue, value, opacity, texture, and crisp edge behavior constant. An explicitly protected primary person using `face-locked, body-flexible, no-source-composite` may remain an exception and is excluded from the crowd fill.
- **Global secondary-person lock:** every visible human figure other than the explicitly designated primary person is a secondary person and must use exactly one shared opaque flat fill color across the entire image, whether or not the figures form an obvious crowd. Keep hue, value, opacity, texture, and crisp edge behavior constant. No secondary face, garment detail, photographic texture, tonal modeling, gradient, hatch, wash, or individual color is permitted.
- **Nonliving duality:** every major non-architectural engineered or landscape region should visibly combine one calm photographic recognition region with one contiguous abstract reconstruction region. Architecture follows the dedicated `Architectural Partial-Sketch Deconstruction` rules below: approximately 70% photographic reality and 20–35% culturally appropriate colored architectural sketch, with 30% preferred.
- **Boundary discipline:** for non-architectural regions, place real/abstract transitions on source-grounded seams—structural joints, perspective axes, shorelines, currents, ridges, slopes, shadow edges, or occlusion boundaries. Architectural transitions follow the dedicated organic sketch-boundary rules and must not become hard rectangular masks.
- **Vocabulary restraint:** use one primary abstract medium and at most one supporting medium across the composition. Reuse their mark density, edge behavior, and value hierarchy consistently. Contrast should come from reality versus abstraction, not from stacking many unrelated styles.
- **High-line-density forced abstraction:** for non-architectural objects, compress repeated lines into broad unequal planes and sparse structural marks. For architecture, do not replace repeated detail with generic planes by default: preserve its geometry and transition selected connected regions into culturally appropriate colored architectural sketch lines, washes, construction marks, and unfinished color while retaining window placement, roof geometry, columns, façade proportions, and perspective.
- **Tiered abstract coverage:** materially reconstruct `light` at 30–40%, `medium` at 45–60% (default upper-stage target), `strong` at 65–85%, and `extreme` at 85–100% of the lower panel's visible artwork area. The extreme lower panel must use broad semantic color planes, sparse structural/contour lines, paper fields, and simplified silhouettes, with almost no photographic microdetail. Global grading, desaturation, blur, haze, posterization, paper/canvas texture, grain, sharpening, or whole-frame painterly filters do not count.
- **Extreme lower-panel human reduction:** in the lower panel, every person—including the primary person—must be reduced to a simplified silhouette, flat mass, or disappear into a grouped semantic plane. No face, skin, hands, anatomy, clothing construction, garment folds, fabric texture, hair detail, or individual photographic shading may remain. The primary face-lock applies to the upper panel and source comparison, not to preserving facial detail in the extreme lower abstraction.
- **Extreme lower-panel palette lock:** the lower panel must use `robot-dreams-logic` as a selected palette, not merely as an optional candidate. Use 4–6 softened colors, warm/cool opposition, 2–3 value steps per semantic mass, charcoal/deep blue instead of pure black, and at most one concentrated accent. Reject naturalistic color scattering, random neon, realistic skin tones, global grayscale, and generic painterly color treatment.
- **Multi-region transformation:** distribute counted abstraction across at least two major nonliving semantic regions—for example architecture plus ground, water plus terrain, shoreline plus supporting buildings, or road plus built fixtures. A single large flat sky or background field cannot satisfy the requirement by itself. Fully photographic living subjects are exempt from abstraction but do not reduce the coverage target for the remaining composition.
- **Photographic-corridor cap:** no single continuous environmental region retaining photographic microdetail may exceed 35% of the full artwork area. Break oversized photographic corridors through semantic reconstruction, not blur, texture overlays, or arbitrary deletion.
- **Architectural hierarchy lock:** identify one primary architectural hero when a dominant landmark or building is present. If no unique hero can be reliably identified, select one building or one tightly related architectural group by compositional harmony: visual weight, placement, scale, silhouette clarity, light, color relationship, depth, and contribution to the eye path. Record this as `selected-hero-by-compositional-harmony`. Apply the 70/30 colored architectural sketch treatment only to the selected hero, in one concentrated contiguous zone. This is an unbuilt-state deconstruction: interrupt selected structural completion cues so the area feels unresolved or not yet constructed, rather than merely covered with lines. Treat all other buildings, city towers, background façades, and non-hero urban structures as secondary architecture: reduce them directly to quiet linear colored elements, simplified source-derived silhouettes, or restrained line bands. Do not give every building the hero's sketch treatment.
- **Architectural presence lock:** preserve the primary hero's source location, footprint, silhouette, depth, viewpoint, perspective, façade geometry, window placement, roof geometry, column positions, and proportions. Secondary architecture must retain only enough source location, skyline rhythm, overlap, depth, and directional silhouette to support scene recognition. Architecture may not be replaced by unrelated structures, but non-hero background buildings may be aggressively reduced to linear colored abstraction rather than retained photographically.
- **Disposable support fixtures — zero photographic retention by default:** classify every non-hero bench, chair, stool, seat, cushion, sofa, low wall used as seating, table, or comparable furniture/support surface as disposable. Rebuild it completely in one abstract language. Integrate the person above it while preserving anatomy, weight, and contact; garment or body edges may blend into the artwork, but the fixture retains no photographic material unless explicitly protected.
- **Ground-plane abstraction lock:** every visible pavement, promenade, road, platform, terrace, or designed ground plane must contain one contiguous non-photographic reconstruction zone. Preserve perspective and spatial function, but compress paving units, stone texture, seams, and surface noise into broad semantic planes, sparse perspective lines, omissions, or a restrained shared medium. A photographic ground with only a color grade, blur, grain, or texture overlay fails.
- **Primary-face perceptual fidelity:** preserve approximately 95% of the primary person's recognizable facial identity by visual comparison, not raw pixel equality. This is a facial-identity target, not a 95% restoration of the whole person, body contour, garment, pose details, or source pixels. Lock face shape and proportions, eye spacing and gaze, brow, nose, mouth, expression, eyewear, hairline, ears, jaw, facial hair, and distinctive asymmetries. Small shifts in color, lighting, texture, or edge treatment are allowed when identity remains immediately recognizable and the artwork becomes more coherent.
- **Face-locked, body-flexible integration:** treat the face as the protected high-fidelity region. Preserve expression, gaze, semantic pose, overall body coherence, and meaningful contact relationships, but allow clothing, hair outside the identity-critical hairline, shoulders, arms, legs, shoes, body contour, peripheral anatomy, local light, material, and color to be regenerated, stylized, simplified, or mildly deformed. The priority order is: facial identity; expression/gaze; pose and body coherence; absence of compositing artifacts; scene integration; artistic coherence; garment/body-edge fidelity; exact source pixels.
- **No Source-Person Reinsertion:** never preserve or repair a person by cutting the original person, face, body, garment, or local photographic region out of the source image and compositing it back into the generated artwork. Do not use source compositing, source-pixel restoration, cutout reinsertion, face patching, body patching, or local source patches as identity-repair methods. Identity preservation must be achieved through generation or localized editing, not source-pixel reinsertion. If the face is insufficiently recognizable, regenerate or locally re-edit the face/person region; do not paste the original face or person back. A slightly stylized or imperfect body edge is preferable to any accidental compositing artifact.
- **Intentional boundary versus compositing failure:** an intentional thin white sticker outline, paper-cut silhouette edge, graphic contour, controlled editorial halo, or cut-paper treatment is acceptable when it is uniform, deliberate, and integrated with the visual language. Misaligned cutouts, duplicated contours, shifted person layers, double edges, partial body offsets, mismatched source patches, body/background seams caused by compositing, pasted-face artifacts, pasted-person artifacts, and floating source-photo fragments are prohibited.
- **95% identity rubric:** assess the face at matched scale and normal viewing size. Pass only when the face is immediately identifiable and no major facial identity carrier is materially wrong. Treat the percentage as an art-direction target for facial identity, not a biometric calculation or whole-person pixel target. If uncertain, compare source and final side by side and correct the smallest failing facial feature through localized generation or editing. Never restore the whole person or paste a source region to solve facial drift.
- **Visual-master priority:** the strongest artistically coherent result is the delivery candidate. Never replace a successful image with a pixelated, posterized, coarsely quantized, lower-resolution, generically masked, or separately recreated fallback in pursuit of technical identity equality.
- This living-subject rule does not apply to non-individual continuous scenery such as sky, water, terrain, mountain, shore, or a grass field treated as one ground mass; these are nonliving scene structures and should carry the controlled half-real/half-abstract relationship.
- Use generation or localized editing only, according to which method best preserves facial identity without weakening the artwork. Source compositing and deterministic source-person restoration are prohibited, including as a fallback or emergency repair. Do not require deterministic full-person restoration.
- Do not invent facial anatomy, provenance, locations, quotations, or capture dates. Preserve the hue identity of living and natural regions—skin, people, animals, sky, clouds, water, vegetation, and natural terrain. Adjust them only semantically and within a plausible neighboring range. Record deliberate color displacement of human-built regions in the palette map.
- **Protected-nature contrast routing:** diagnose natural, built, and ground masses together. If separation is weak, route contrast through coherent built-region color notes or the architectural sketch medium while preserving architectural identity, material logic, and light direction. Do not recolor architecture as scattered patches.
- Keep each large color field within one semantic region. Do not wash across architecture, sky, ground, water, people, or another major subject.
- **Unified-sky field:** treat the entire visible sky as one continuous natural color field. Preserve one source-derived dominant hue family across the sky; allow only smooth same-family value, temperature, or atmospheric gradients and source-grounded cloud transitions. Never split an abstracted sky into multiple discrete large polygons, cut-paper blocks, ribbons, wedges, or unrelated color fields. Reserve hard-edged plane fragmentation for built structures and designed surfaces, not open sky.
- Keep exact final lettering legible. Add it deterministically when image generation cannot render it reliably.
- **Source-derived canvas geometry:** preserve the user's original image geometry first and adapt the poster around it. Read the source width, height, and aspect ratio before allocating any band or abstract section. The source dimensions are the composition basis; fixed `1800×3000`, `3000×1800`, `3:5`, or `5:3` master canvases are disabled. Fixed export dimensions may exist only as proportional long-edge limits, bounding caps, or metadata-failure fallbacks and must never control source-image geometry.
- **Proportional scaling only:** never stretch, squeeze, widen, flatten, or independently scale the source on x and y. Prefer contain-style full-framing preservation. Use cover-style placement only when the crop is minor, source-aware, compositionally justified, and does not damage source identity. Preserve full source framing over aggressive crop.
- **Default five-band editorial field:** unless the user explicitly requests `fullbleed`, derive the canvas from source width, source height, source aspect ratio, and equal panel heights, in this fixed order: narrow warm-white date band; upper photographic-led panel; narrow black title band; lower rectangular abstraction panel; narrow black subtitle band. The date band sits above the upper panel, the title band touches the upper panel's bottom edge, and the subtitle band touches the lower panel's bottom edge. Bands are narrow and must never expand into a large blank area. Upper and lower panel boxes share equal external width and height; only the upper panel's internal content contour may be non-rectangular.
- **Content-boundary semantics:** derive one dominant outer contour and at most one quieter supporting contour from visible evidence: canopy/branch fall for foliage, roofline/façade/street perspective for architecture, ridge/slope/dune for terrain, tide/current/coast/wake for water, cloud flow for sky-led scenes, or pose/gesture/shadow/contact-ground for person-led scenes. Dry brush, ink depletion, watercolor bloom, and paper-fiber disappearance may soften this structure but cannot replace it. Alpha or an explicit mask implements the design; color-based edge-keying never chooses it.
- **Upper-panel color-preservation lock:** never derive the upper panel's alpha by removing a sampled paper/ground color when that color also occurs inside the artwork. Warm ivory architecture, white clouds, pale skin, highlights, water glints, and paper-like reconstructed planes must remain fully opaque and color-identical after compositing. Prefer an authored semantic alpha mask (painted from the outer contour only) or generate the upper panel with a true transparent background. If neither is available, keep the upper panel RGB and use a conservative outer contour mask; do not use `remove_chroma_key.py`, `--background-removal edge-key`, or any flood-fill keyed by a sampled color. Always compare the isolated upper RGB against the composited upper panel at matched scale; reject any hue/value shift or pale-region deletion.

### Default strategies

- Apply a natural/built color boundary. Keep people, animals, sky, clouds, water, vegetation, and natural terrain source-faithful, allowing only restrained semantic shifts in brightness, saturation, temperature, or atmospheric value. Permit stronger palette displacement on human-built architecture, paving, walls, furniture, fixtures, roads, vehicles, and designed objects when it strengthens the proposition and separates the poster from the source. Treat clothing as designed material: shift it moderately only when identity and hierarchy remain intact.
- When the dominant natural, architecture, and ground colors cluster too closely, do not accept a low-impact factual palette by default. Compare at least three candidate interventions—shift the hero architecture, shift the designed ground/fixtures, or split the adjustment across both—and choose the smallest coherent built-region change that creates clear figure/ground separation. Favor complementary temperature, useful value separation, or controlled saturation contrast; avoid arbitrary neon, equal-intensity competition, and hue changes that weaken landmark identity.
- Inventory every living subject before editing. Detect clusters first and assign each cluster one shared `fully abstract` language; then assign isolated non-primary subjects `fully photographic` or `fully abstract` and the primary person `face-locked, body-flexible, no-source-composite`. Keep facial identity, semantic pose, and biological continuity coherent while allowing controlled regeneration and transitions through clothing and peripheral body edges.
- **Auto-candidate `robot-dreams-logic` palette:** always honor an explicit user request first; otherwise evaluate this palette automatically. Mark it `candidate` when the source has weak or scattered palette hierarchy, human-built/designed regions occupy roughly 25% or more of the artwork, the scene supports restrained everyday, travel, urban, interior, seasonal, nostalgic, or quietly emotional treatment, and no documentary or color-critical constraint dominates. Compare two semantic palette plans before generation: A=`shifted`, B=`robot-dreams-logic`. Select B automatically only when it clearly improves at least two of palette hierarchy, emotional coherence, and subject/ground hierarchy without weakening identity, landmark recognition, material logic, or protected natural hues; otherwise record `fallback` and use A. Set `off` for primarily natural scenes with insufficient built color levers, already strong/coherent source palettes, documentary or historical color requirements, uniforms/rituals, brand/product color fidelity, or high-energy/neon intentions. When selected, borrow general color logic only—never a frame, character, composition, or exact sampled palette. Use 4–6 softened colors, a warm low-chroma ground, one restrained cool family, one restrained warm family, charcoal instead of pure black, and at most one concentrated emotional accent such as coral, tomato red, mustard, or clear blue. Prefer flat or semi-flat regions with 2–3 value steps. Keep skin and protected natural/living regions source-neighbor plausible and route stronger shifts through complete human-built regions.
- **Monochromatic-scene palette expansion for `robot-dreams-logic`:** before palette selection, diagnose whether the source is effectively monochromatic: one hue dominates most of the frame, warm/cool separation is weak, major semantic masses have low chromatic contrast, or a source-faithful edit would read only as minor color correction. When triggered, palette fidelity is no longer the primary objective; prefer `palette_mode: expressive` or a deliberate shifted palette. When no stronger user-specified palette reference overrides it, use `robot-dreams-logic` as the preferred cinematic color-logic reference, interpreting it rather than imitating it literally. Candidate colors may include muted peach, dusty pink, soft lilac, desaturated teal, softened blue, creamy yellow, and warm off-white highlights, arranged as restrained pastel warm/cool opposition. Do not merely intensify the dominant source hue: a blue-dominated image must gain a controlled secondary temperature family through suitable semantic regions. Route new color primarily through atmospheric gradients, reflected light, wet surfaces, haze, clouds, architectural planes, designed ground, or non-identity-critical illumination. Preserve scene structure, lighting direction, semantic boundaries, and subject identity even when color departs substantially from the source. Color displacement may be visibly stronger than the original when strict source fidelity would make the intervention insignificant, but every added hue must remain coherent with the scene's light, material, atmosphere, or reflected-color logic. Reject results where the only visible change is saturation, white balance, contrast, a global tint, or minor blue/orange grading. A successful result must produce an immediately perceptible palette transformation while preserving the scene's emotional identity and separating previously merged semantic masses. Record the diagnosis, selected palette mode, warm/cool routing, and rejected conservative alternative in `robot_dreams_auto_assessment`.
- Use scene-semantic brightness. Favor calm middle-to-lower values for quiet, historic, intimate, autumnal, or overcast scenes; allow brighter values for sunny, snowy, seaside, summer, or celebratory scenes.
- Build a few unequal semantic masses. Use an echo color only when it carries a necessary structural or conceptual turn; otherwise set it to `none`. Never add accent marks merely because a source hue is available.
- Partially deconstruct prominent tall nonliving anchors while retaining decisive silhouette, spatial role, and one or two identifying features. Make the intervention legible through one contiguous reconstructed zone, large omissions, and a few hierarchical marks; do not substitute all-over sketch texture for deconstruction.
- When one architecture or landmark is conspicuously dominant, establish it as the single hero building. When no building is clearly dominant, choose one building or coherent architectural group that best supports the composition's visual harmony and eye path, and treat it as the temporary hero. Apply the same-building 70/30 colored-sketch treatment only to one concentrated contiguous region of that hero. Deconstruct that region as if construction has stopped: preserve the main mass and alignment, but interrupt selected shells, bays, joints, openings, or surface completion with omissions, unfinished planes, broken structural continuations, visible construction axes, and paper/sky intrusions consistent with the building's perspective. Do not merely draw lines on an otherwise complete surface. Do not scatter sketch lines around the full perimeter or distribute small marks across unrelated façade edges. Supporting buildings, distant towers, and urban background masses receive linear colored abstraction, quiet silhouettes, or simplified line bands; they do not receive independent 20–35% sketch zones.
- Fully abstract disposable support fixtures by default, even when their texture appears visually quiet or helps explain contact. Inventory every bench, chair, stool, seat, cushion, sofa, low seating wall, table, and comparable support before editing; assign the entire fixture a zero-photographic-retention treatment. Preserve contact logic through abstract geometry and a coherently generated or locally edited person above it, never through a photographic patch of the fixture or source-person reinsertion.
- Omit or strongly merge non-architectural source elements when they fragment the intended semantic masses without supporting recognition or the artistic proposition. Never apply this omission rule to recognizable architecture: architectural identity, geometry, and structural continuity take priority.
- Give a dominant low-variety region contained value bands or material rhythm; keep construction-line language mainly on architecture and engineered structures.
- Run the architectural hierarchy and alignment inventory before generation and again after the first pass. Select one identifiable hero or, when necessary, one compositionally selected hero by visual harmony; define one concentrated sketch zone inside its body; and route all secondary architecture to linear colored abstraction or quiet silhouettes. Check the hero's silhouette, façade geometry, window placement, roof geometry, columns, proportions, vanishing direction, and structural continuity. A real edge may continue as a drawn edge, but no duplicated, shifted, perimeter-scattered, or decorative contour is acceptable.
- Reduce clustered pedestrians and nonessential secondary pedestrians to one coherent single-tone silhouette system. Use exactly one shared opaque flat fill across all such people in the image; preserve only group range, density, proportion, pose rhythm, direction, spacing, and necessary overlap. Use crisp hard edges, merge collisions into clean union masses or open a narrow paper-colored separation gap, and allow no face, garment detail, tonal depth steps, transparency, hatch, paper grain, sketch texture, ink/watercolor wash, photographic islands, internal modeling, or edge bleed.
- Keep hierarchy classes visually distinct. Do not give secondary people the structural line language used for architecture. Assign photographic texture to identity anchors, sparse structural marks to selected built forms, and solid fill-only silhouettes to crowds.

### Architectural Partial-Sketch Deconstruction

Activate this treatment whenever any clearly recognizable architectural element is visible, even when architecture is not the main subject and occupies only a small part of the frame. This includes buildings, houses, towers, churches, temples, palaces, pavilions, façades, roofs, arches, columns, windows, gates, bridges with architectural characteristics, and urban structures.

Before editing, classify each visible architectural element as `chinese-east-asian`, `western`, or `mixed-source`. Match the sketch language to the building itself. Chinese/East Asian architecture uses East Asian line drawing, restrained pigment-like accents, translucent washes, intentional blank space, expressive roof-line studies, and hand-rendered structural detail. Western architecture uses architectural watercolor, colored pencil, ink-and-wash, graphite construction lines, perspective studies, loose façade color blocking, and culturally coherent stone, earth, brick, or aged-color notes. If traditions coexist, treat each building in its own language. Never mix Chinese and Western architectural abstraction merely for visual variety.

The default architectural balance applies only to the primary architectural hero: approximately 70% photographic reality and 20–35% colored hand-drawn architectural study, with 30% preferred, measured over the visible hero body. The study region must communicate partial non-completion through structural omissions and interrupted construction, not merely surface linework. Preserve enough completed mass for the hero to remain recognizable, but allow selected parts to appear unresolved, absent, transparent, or still on paper. Secondary/background architecture does not receive this balance; reduce it directly to linear colored elements, quiet source-derived silhouettes, or restrained line bands. The hero must still read first as the same building. This treatment is a change in construction/rendering state, not replacement: never insert another building, foreign architectural component, collage fragment, geometric block replacement, surreal architectural mutation, or different architectural style.

Use colored sketch as the required default medium for the primary hero, but use it to depict an unfinished construction state rather than a finished building covered with lines. It may combine architectural pencil lines, construction axes, loose perspective lines, colored pencil, watercolor, gouache-like notes, pastel marks, ink outlines, translucent washes, unfinished paint, paper-like negative space, incomplete planes, interrupted contours, missing joints, and partially resolved structural studies. Select one concentrated contiguous architectural zone based on visual logic—such as a connected façade side, roof section, tower segment, or structural bay group. Within that zone, use at least two deconstruction signals: a missing or omitted plane, an interrupted contour, an unresolved opening/joint, a visible construction axis, a transparent/paper intrusion, or a color block that stops before completing the form. Do not scatter marks around the perimeter, create isolated decorative patches, or convert multiple unrelated zones. Never convert a uniform rectangular slice of the canvas. Secondary architecture instead becomes linear colored abstraction with sparse source-derived outlines, verticals, horizontals, perspective strokes, and simplified skyline bands.

Structural alignment is mandatory. Preserve perspective, silhouette, façade geometry, window placement, roof geometry, column positions, architectural proportions, vanishing direction, and structural continuity. Real edges may continue as drawn edges; real windows may become sketched windows in the same positions; roof tiles may become roof-line studies without changing the roof. Never duplicate and shift an architectural layer, paste façade fragments, offset windows, double roof outlines, or create a detached overlay. Prioritize structural continuity, clean alignment, readable architecture, and intentional hand-drawn abstraction over literal pixel reconstruction.

Use an organic transition: dissolving edges, brush-like boundaries, irregular pigment, unfinished drawing edges, watercolor bleed, sparse construction lines appearing gradually, or photography fading into paper-like negative space. Avoid hard rectangular masks inside the building unless explicitly required by the composition. Do not apply the treatment to people, faces, trees, sky, vehicles, roads, mountains, or water merely because architecture is present; non-architectural elements may interact softly with the boundary only for integration.

When another rule conflicts with this treatment, preserve priority in this order: (1) original architectural identity, (2) perspective and structural alignment, (3) approximately 70% photographic reality, (4) approximately 30% colored architectural sketch, (5) culturally appropriate sketch language, (6) organic transition, (7) overall editorial harmony. Avoid Chinese-Western hybrid motifs without source justification, full-building sketch conversion, 50% or greater sketch conversion by default, monochrome blueprint default, hard Photoshop-mask appearance, duplicated contours, misregistration, invented towers/roofs/arches/windows, unrelated ornament, and abstract geometry that destroys readability.
- **Cloud metaphor:** default `off`. Enable only when the user requests it or an existing cloud formation makes the metaphor unusually natural. Never add clouds merely to create a motif.
- **Cinematic title:** default `off`. Enable when requested or when a released-film connection is exceptionally strong and verifiable; otherwise use user wording or a source-grounded original title.
- **Illustrated/anime portrait:** explicit-only. Never select it solely because a face is front-facing. Enable only when the user requests illustration/anime treatment or clearly accepts identity reinterpretation.

## Abstraction Strength

- **`light` — 30–40%:** retain most identity/documentary evidence, but reconstruct at least two identity-safe semantic regions. Prefer for sensitive portraits, complex contact points, or fragile landmarks.
- **`medium` — 45–60% (default):** retain selected photographic anchors while replacing substantial portions of at least two major regions with semantic masses, drawing, paint, ink, paper omission, or partial landmark reconstruction. The first generated pass must target this range, not the 30% floor.
- **`strong` — 65–85%:** rely on large masses, paper omissions, broken planes, displaced axes, drawing/paint systems, and sparse photographic anchors. Use for robust silhouettes, scenery, distant figures, or explicit requests.

Use only after explicit user intent for illustration/anime treatment and accepted identity reinterpretation. ## Output Modes

### Two-Image Collage — Default

Unless the user explicitly requests `fullbleed` or another output mode, `processed` uses the fixed five-band editorial collage:

```text
[ warm-white date band ]
[ upper non-rectangular interwoven image ]
[ black main-title band ]
[ lower rectangular highly abstract image ]
[ black subtitle band ]
```

This is an integrated diptych with editorial text bands, not a before/after comparison, postcard, social-media grid, or two unrelated blocks. The upper panel is the primary authored image: photographic-led, source-semantic, richer, and bounded by a free-edged non-rectangular contour. It is never the untouched original. The lower panel is a strict rectangle and a materially stronger abstraction of the same scene, subject, structure, or proposition. It must be a deeper abstraction layer rather than a crop, duplicate, or lightly altered copy.

The panels must share the same source, proposition, palette family, and compatible abstract vocabulary while differing in abstraction depth: `upper = interweaving`, `lower = abstraction intensification`. The upper panel carries the main identity, landmark, and scene-reading burden. The lower panel is supporting and must not dominate.

**Original-image exclusion is non-negotiable:** the uploaded photograph is an analysis, geometry, palette, identity, and metadata reference only. It must not appear as a panel, inset, thumbnail, comparison strip, background layer, or visible source fragment in the finished poster. Both visible image sections must be newly generated or coherently edited interpretations.

Before constructing this system, read `original.width` and `original.height`, compute the source aspect ratio, classify orientation, and derive the complete collage canvas from that source geometry plus relative section heights. Do not create a fixed poster canvas first and force the source into it. Both generated stages preserve the source ratio, framing logic, subject placement, landmark identity, perspective, and major spatial structure through proportional contain-style scaling. Width and height parameters are export caps, not composition dimensions.

### Upper Panel — Interwoven Main Image

The upper panel is not the untouched original photograph. Generate or edit the complete scene as one coherent image with a light-to-medium intervention while keeping photography as the dominant read.

- Preserve facial identity, expression, gaze, pose, landmark identity, framing, perspective, light direction, and major object relationships.
- Permit unified editorial color, paper/gouache texture, simplified crowd language, reduced microdetail, broad designed-ground planes, and selective architectural reconstruction.
- Keep enough photographic surface, tonal depth, material evidence, and natural light that the viewer reads it first as a transformed photograph rather than a flat illustration.
- Do not create it by placing cut-out source people, faces, bodies, garments, or local source patches over generated content. Generate or locally edit the whole scene coherently.
- Apply the same palette and material family used below so the two stages feel authored together.
- Use a source-semantic, free-edged, non-rectangular outer contour; do not render the upper panel as a clean rectangular box.
- Preserve the main recognition anchors and allow the upper panel to carry the primary identity and landmark burden.

### Lower Panel — Rectangular Extreme Abstraction

The lower panel materially advances the same scene into **extreme semantic abstraction**. It is a strict rectangular image field and should read primarily as a composed system of broad color planes, sparse contour/structural lines, paper fields, and simplified silhouettes. Preserve only the minimum anchors needed for continuity: the Opera House shell rhythm or landmark mass, horizon/water direction, main figure placement as a single flat silhouette, major ground axis, and one or two source-specific structural relationships. Remove photographic microdetail almost completely.

The lower panel must not visibly contain human faces, skin, hands, anatomy, garment construction, clothing folds, fabric texture, hair detail, or recognizable individual bodies. The primary person becomes a single simplified silhouette or dark semantic mass in the lower panel; identity protection applies to the upper panel only. Secondary people become tiny unified silhouettes or disappear into grouped masses. Seats, cushions, furniture, paving, windows, and repeated architectural detail become broad planes, omissions, or sparse lines rather than recognizable objects.

The lower panel uses a strict `robot-dreams-logic` palette lock. Use a restrained warm/cool system with softened blue, warm ivory, muted peach/coral or tomato, dusty mauve/lilac, charcoal/deep blue, and warm off-white as appropriate to the source. Route colors through complete semantic masses, not scattered accents. Do not use naturalistic local colors, realistic skin colors, photographic shadows, global black-and-white treatment, random neon, or a generic painterly filter. The palette must feel like a deliberate robot-dreams color construction: 4–6 coherent colors, 2–3 value steps per mass, warm/cool hierarchy, charcoal instead of pure black, and at most one concentrated accent.

The lower panel normally uses the `extreme` tier, stronger than `strong`: target approximately 85–100% non-photographic semantic reconstruction. It must not merely zoom into, repeat, or remain too photographic compared with the upper panel. If a lower-panel element can be identified mainly by clothing, facial features, material texture, or photographic shading, abstract it further.

**Progression is non-negotiable:** the upper section must be visibly transformed but photographic-led; the lower section must be visibly extreme, graphic, and semantically distilled. Reject untouched-original upper sections, an upper and lower panel with similar rendering density, a lower panel with recognizable human anatomy/clothing, or two unrelated interpretations.

- **Landscape source:** keep the upper photographic region wider and shallower, permit a more horizontally extended abstract motif, and preserve horizontal movement.
- **Portrait source:** make the photographic region visibly taller, preserve portrait reading, avoid a shallow horizontal strip, and organize the lower abstraction vertically, centrally, or along a strong vertical axis. Preserve necessary headroom and footroom around tall subjects.
- **Near-square source:** balance the photo and abstract sections without mechanically choosing the landscape profile; let the abstract axis follow the source's strongest relationship.

For portrait sources, prioritize source integrity over template regularity. Mild cropping is allowed, but do not stretch, compress, severely horizontal-crop, or unnaturally truncate a person, building, tower, tree, doorway, column, mountain, or other tall subject. The lower abstract motif must retain the source's vertical axis, height hierarchy, and subject-to-blank-space relationship rather than becoming a low horizontal strip.

**Orientation adaptation is non-negotiable:** keep the five-band system, but adapt panel proportions and geometry to source orientation. Portrait images must remain portrait-led compositions; landscape images may extend horizontally; near-square images should be balanced without mechanically forcing a landscape template. Never stretch or compress a panel to fit a fixed template.

### Typography in Two-Image Collage

Use the fixed five-band typography layout: date centered in the top warm-white band using restrained dark serif text; main title centered in the black band immediately below the upper panel using warm-white text; subtitle centered in the black band immediately below the lower panel using warm-white text. Date, title, and subtitle are required in `processed` mode unless the user explicitly requests a copy-free result. Each band is a narrow strip, approximately 4–6% of the complete canvas height, with no extra inter-panel gap. Typography must remain legible and subordinate; never allow automatic text placement to create a large blank band or move copy into the image panels.

**Source geometry is non-negotiable:** preserve the user's original image geometry first; adapt the poster around it. Fixed export size may exist, but fixed composition size must not control the source image.

Selectable modes:

- **`processed` — default:** orientation-adaptive photographic-led partial abstraction above and stronger abstraction below.
- **`fullbleed` — edge-to-edge treatment:** one complete rectangular interpretation.

When the user asks for a poster or processed result without specifying a mode, use `processed`.

## Identity Review

For every visible primary face, inspect the final artwork beside the source at matched face scale and at normal viewing size. Use this order:

1. Confirm immediate recognizability.
2. Compare face shape/proportions, gaze and eye spacing, brows, nose, mouth and expression, eyewear, hairline, ears, jaw, facial hair, and distinctive asymmetries.
3. Require no major identity carrier to be materially wrong. Treat approximately 95% fidelity as a perceptual art-direction target, not a biometric calculation or pixel-difference threshold.
4. Permit neighboring changes in skin color, lighting, texture, edge softness, and material treatment when they support the final palette and do not alter identity.
5. Permit broader transformation of clothing, garment edges, fabric texture, peripheral hair, body-edge transitions, and local silhouette details when anatomy, gesture, and contact remain credible.
6. Prefer localized generation or editing when one facial feature drifts. Do not use source blending, face patches, body patches, or source-person reinsertion. Regenerate the person region or the complete image if necessary.

The bundled `verify_identity_lock.py` is not an identity-repair method. Do not use it to justify exact pixel preservation, source-person reinsertion, or compositing; facial recognizability and visual coherence take priority over exact body pixels.

## Quality Gate

- **`S01 proposition-drift`:** every major intervention supports the source-grounded artistic proposition; no decorative motif competes with it.
- **`R01 recognition-loss`:** the source remains recognizable through large relationships and preserved evidence rather than copied microdetail.
- **`A01 insufficient-abstraction`:** counted reconstructed area meets the chosen tier—light 30–40%, medium 45–60%, strong 65–85%—using conservative area estimates that exclude filter-like tonal/texture changes.
- **`A02 single-region-abstraction`:** counted abstraction spans at least two major semantic regions and is not satisfied only by a large sky/background field.
- **`A03 photographic-corridor`:** no continuous environmental photographic region exceeds 35% of full artwork area.
- **`C01 mass-fragmentation`:** the scene reads as a few coherent semantic masses.
- **`C02 boundary-crossing`:** color fields remain inside their semantic regions.
- **`C04 sky-fragmentation`:** the sky reads as one continuous source-derived hue field with only smooth same-family atmospheric variation; no discrete large color blocks, hard wedges, or cut-paper segmentation divide it.
- **`C03 accent-scatter`:** the source-derived echo color is concentrated, visible, and subordinate.
- **`C05 weak-palette-separation`:** when dominant natural, hero-built, and ground/designed masses begin with weak color separation, the result uses one or two coherent built-region color interventions to create a clearly stronger hue, value, or temperature hierarchy while keeping protected natural/living hues plausible.
- **`L01 landmark-generic`:** the primary hero must retain decisive identifiers while converting one concentrated 20–35% region into a culturally appropriate colored sketch; secondary architecture may be reduced to linear colored elements.
- **`L02 hierarchy-conflict`:** one dominant hero building remains unmistakable; supporting architecture is materially quieter, simpler, lower-contrast, and does not compete through literal detail or a second incompatible style.
- **`L03 architecture-erasure`:** every recognizable architectural element remains present at its original location, depth, footprint, silhouette, viewpoint, and perspective. Restore any building replaced by blur, smearing, blankness, vegetation, sky, a false opening, collage fragment, or invented structure.
- **`P01 identity-drift`:** the person mode follows identity sensitivity and user intent; anatomy, skin, face, pose, and contact evidence remain coherent where required.
- **`P00 identity-fidelity-loss` (blocking):** the primary face is not immediately recognizable, or a major identity carrier—face proportion, gaze/eye spacing, brow, nose, mouth/expression, eyewear, hairline, ears, jaw, facial hair, or distinctive asymmetry—is materially wrong. Correct the smallest failing feature and reassess. Exact pixels, identical color, and unchanged facial texture are not required.
- **`Q00 artistic-quality-regression` (blocking):** a technically safer or more source-faithful revision is materially worse as an artwork through pixelation, posterization, coarse quantization, generic masking, pasted-person edges, lower resolution, weakened palette, or lost semantic structure. Keep the artistically stronger version and solve only the specific identity issue.
- **`P02 identity-integration-failure`:** the person appears accidentally pasted on, haloed, rectangularly patched, mismatched in sharpness/grain/light, anatomically broken, or incoherently half-rendered. A deliberate, uniform sticker outline, paper-cut edge, graphic contour, or controlled editorial halo is allowed; accidental seams, duplicated edges, shifted layers, partial body offsets, mismatched source patches, pasted-face artifacts, pasted-body artifacts, and mixed-resolution human fragments are blocking failures. Regenerate the person region or complete image; never repair by source compositing.
- **`P03 source-person-reinsertion` (blocking):** any original person, face, body, garment, or local photographic region has been cut out and pasted back into the generated artwork, whether as a default composite, identity patch, body patch, source-pixel restoration, or emergency repair. Facial identity must be preserved through generation or localized editing. Exact source-pixel fidelity is always lower priority than facial identity, anatomical/visual coherence, absence of compositing artifacts, scene integration, and artistic coherence.
- **`B01 biological-language-split`:** every non-human living individual or connected organism uses one coherent treatment across its complete visible body. No tree mixes photographic trunk/branches with abstract foliage; no animal or bird mixes photographic anatomy with graphic body regions.
